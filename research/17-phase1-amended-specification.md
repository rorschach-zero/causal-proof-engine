## 1. Objective

Reduce scientific and engineering uncertainty about the measurement apparatus with synthetic data only, so
that a later, separately authorised Phase 2 can register real events under a protocol whose validity has been
demonstrated adversarially. No network, no providers, no broker, no Jev client, no hosting decision, no learned
components, no real event.

## 3. Scope (build)

1. **Schemas.** `EventSpec` with typed resolution predicate (source, record selector, field, comparator, value,
   not-before, not-after), mandatory `cluster_id` (a singleton is declared explicitly as a cluster with one
   member; there is no implicit default, RC-53), `predicate_kind ∈ {milestone-deadline, terminal-falsifier}`,
   four timestamps (occurrence, publication, receipt, adjudication), latency window, and the adjudicated
   `knowable_at`: the earliest time at which the outcome was publicly determinable (the publication time, or the
   occurrence time when the occurrence is itself a public act), recorded with its evidence and audited (RC-52). A terminal-falsifier predicate also declares
   `falsifier_observable_from`, the earliest time at which its source can publish the falsifier (OD5). A member's
   **earliest-knowable bound** e_i is its `not_before` for a milestone-deadline predicate and min(`not_before`,
   `falsifier_observable_from`) for a terminal-falsifier predicate; it is fixed at registration, depends on no
   outcome, and is the one bound that every rule below guarding against a known outcome uses (RC-138).
   `Forecast` with one scored point probability, declared ambiguity interval (low ≤ point ≤ high), the **arm
   version id**, prompt and model hashes and evidence ids. `ArmVersion`: an immutable registration record per arm
   and forecaster version, created **before any event is registered under it**, carrying the forecaster id and
   version and the declared **model knowledge cutoff** (RC-98: a date, recorded as an UNVERIFIED provider claim
   with the archived evidence it was read from; `AGENTS.md` §8 requires the cutoff per forecast, and the reference
   from every `Forecast` to its arm version satisfies that). A `Forecast` carries no cutoff of its own and a
   submission with one, or one naming an arm version created after the event's registration, is refused
   (RC-112). A member is eligible for the primary and the monitor only if every arm version's cutoff precedes the
   member's earliest-knowable bound e_i (RC-138; version 10 compared the cutoff with `not_before`, which a falsifier
   observable earlier escapes), checked at event registration against the arm version records, before any forecast
   exists. A member whose adjudicated `knowable_at` is nevertheless at or before an arm's cutoff is an
   **early-knowability breach** (RC-135): it stays in the primary, the monitor and every comparison, scored at its
   least favourable outcome, the Y that minimises its paired increment X_i given the primary-slot forecasts (an
   abstaining arm is scored at the baseline forecast as everywhere), is imputed at both outcomes in the worst-case
   bounds, is outside the VOID cap, and is reported with the breach count and the arm whose cutoff it breaches
   (fixture F-27). Version 10 excluded such a member from the skill statistics. On a milestone-deadline predicate
   only a YES can be knowable before e_i, because a NO is knowable at logical finality (on a terminal-falsifier
   predicate a breach is a NO), so the exclusion was a complete-case selection on the outcome:
   on a null where FULL's loss on a YES is the larger one it raised the primary's rejection rate from 5.2 % to 9.3 %,
   15.2 % and 33.9 % at breach shares of 1 %, 2 % and 4 % of members (from 1.8 % to 3.6 %, 6.5 % and 18.2 % at
   c* = 2.254). Kept at its least favourable outcome the imputed increment is at most the true one; the rate equals
   the no-breach rate on that law and falls below it on a law with forecasts in both directions, so the cost is power
   (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix N). The block statistic is not monotone in a single increment, so this is a simulated
   guarantee on the committed laws, carried as a T-12 family, not a theorem. The alternative Codex named, ending the
   confirmatory claim of every comparison a breach touches, is also selection-free and costs the whole experiment
   for one misdeclared bound; it is recorded for the owner under OD10. A member whose adjudicated `knowable_at` is at or
   before its cluster's registration record is a **registration breach** (RC-139; with cutoff 0, `knowable_at` 5,
   registration 10 and a declared e_i of 20 both registration checks pass and the cutoff rule above sees no breach).
   No forecast on it can be eligible, because a forecast is refused before the registration record (RC-87) and
   excluded at or after t*_k ≤ `knowable_at` (item 3), and t*_k is the earliest `knowable_at` over the cluster's
   members, so the whole cluster has no eligible forecast. The cluster is therefore **outside the scored population**
   of the primary, the monitor and every comparison (RC-143), like a member pre-declared VOID in the registration
   record: its breach status is fixed by a fact that was public when the cluster was registered and before any
   forecast existed, so leaving it out removes no scored value and is the refusal the registration check would have
   made had `knowable_at` been known. It is reported in the candidate log and the VOID audit with the
   registration-breach count by source, is outside the cap, the bounds and every arm's abstention cap, and a source
   whose registration-breach count exceeds its pre-registered bound is withdrawn from new registrations (OD5). Version
   12 kept such a cluster in the primary at X = 0 and called the zeros inert. They are not: a zero moves the cluster
   mean and every block's deviation, so on a 24-block null sample 30 zeros added to a block with sum 11 move t from
   2.167 to 2.261 across c* = 2.254, and a registrar who places 30 such clusters, after the outcomes, in the block
   that maximises t raises null rejection from 4.8 % to 5.3 % (from 1.5 % to 1.9 % at c* = 2.254), while uniformly
   drawn blocks leave it unchanged (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix P). Scored from forecasts made after publication such
   members would select on the outcome directly: on law (a) of RC-135, adding known-NO members raises null rejection
   from 5.2 % to 7.6 %, 12.5 % and 24.5 % at 1 %, 2.4 % and 4.8 % of the cohort (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix O). Leaving
   such clusters out makes the number of scored clusters per block depend on which outcomes were public early; that
   dependence is carried by the supply–shock coordinate of the null class N (OD4), an assumption of the calibration
   and not a theorem.
   A **correction** of a cutoff withdraws the arm version: every
   member registered under it leaves the primary for every comparison involving that arm as one cohort, counted
   against the cap and imputed at ±1, never member by member, and a provider's model change is a new arm version
   with its own pre-registration (RC-96). Version 7 recorded the cutoff on each `Forecast` and let a corrected
   cutoff exclude individual members at adjudication; a date rule applied member by member is outcome-blind in
   its criterion and outcome-dependent in its timing, and choosing when to correct chose which members left: on a
   null with mean-zero increments an outcome-chosen correction month gives 13 % rejection where no correction
   gives 5 % (RC-112; REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix K). **Cohort withdrawal is open only before unblinding (RC-132).**
   Unblinding is the experiment's first outcome-knowable time, the earliest t*_k over the clusters of the primary;
   before it no outcome exists to select on and the cohort rule above applies. A correction recorded at or after it
   withdraws the arm version from further registrations and makes the primary and every comparison involving that
   provider **non-confirmatory** for the experiment; since every provider feeds FULL, the confirmatory conjunction
   ends and is reported as exploratory with the correction. No member, cohort or IUT component leaves the
   confirmatory analysis because of such a correction, so the correction can end a claim but never improve one.
   Version 9 let a post-unblinding correction remove one version's cohort: with twenty versions of 5 % each, inside
   a 5 % cap, correcting the version whose removal helps most raises null rejection from 5.2 % to 12.3 % at the
   nominal cutoff, from 0.7 % to 2.5 % at 2.693 and from 1.9 % to 5.3 % at 2.254 (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix M).
   The alternative, keeping the corrected members in the primary, is also free of selection but scores forecasts
   whose cutoff may postdate their outcomes; it is recorded for OD10. The point-in-time store cannot see the cutoff channel: a model
   trained through February can carry a January outcome in its parameters while every read satisfies
   `available_at` ≤ t_cut. `JevRecord` (inputs, both polarities, model hash, routing result, cost).
   `Capability` (owner-signed: capability, environment, release digest, evidence digest, epoch, nonce,
   expiry). `ResearchOutput` JSON schema for future adapters. `Cluster` registration record.
   *Scoring slot (RC-75, RC-89, RC-96).* `Forecast` records are keyed by (event id, arm, scoring slot). The
   forecaster version is **not** part of the key: it is pre-registered per arm and per event, a submission that
   names another version is refused at submission, and a version change is a new arm that needs its own
   pre-registration (a key that included the version admitted two records per slot and re-opened the RC-75
   channel: with two honest versions of one forecaster the null expected increment is −0.04 when the version is
   fixed in advance and +0.07 when the better one is scored after the outcome; REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix J).
   The EventSpec pre-registers its scoring slots; the default is one slot, filled by the first forecast received
   for the arm, and a second record for an occupied slot is refused at submission. An EventSpec with a revision
   schedule names every slot in advance and names exactly one of them per arm as the **primary slot**, whose
   issuance window and evidence cutoff are the event's own (RC-76); only the primary slot's record supplies
   p_FULL,i and p_baseline,i to the increment, the primary test and the monitor, and every other slot is scored
   and reported as a descriptive secondary, never aggregated into the increment and never chosen after the
   outcome: scoring the revision nearest the outcome turns an honest null expected increment of −0.04 into +0.16
   (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix G). Nothing in the eligibility rules (receipt before t*_k) constrains which eligible
   record is used; only the key does.
   *Logical finality (RC-77, RC-88, RC-105).* For an eventually-by-deadline predicate, `knowable_at` =
   min(publication time, public occurrence time, deadline + declared latency window): the outcome is publicly
   determinable from the earliest of these, and deadline + window, its logical finality, is the latest it can be
   (RC-146: versions 5 to 12 wrote "after the last of these", which on an occurrence public at 90, finality at 110
   and publication at 118 reads 118 instead of 90 and admits a forecast at 100; deterministic, `16` Appendix P), and a forecast received in the gap between deadline + window and the
   source's formal record is not eligible (fixture F-21b: deadline 100, window 10, record received 118, forecast
   received 113; eligible under the version-4 rule, not under this one; REPRODUCED). **The latency window is
   applied to the evidenced publication time** of the source record (the time the archived bytes carry, with the
   observer's `first_observed_at` beside it), never to registry receipt: a record published inside the window
   and received late because the observer was down resolves by its content, and the receipt lag is an
   integrity incident logged separately (version 6 turned that outage into a VOID, RC-105). **The disposition
   at finality is immutable** (RC-88): with no record, a living source and an archived observation of the
   source at or after deadline + window showing no record, the member is NO at deadline + window; with the
   source rule's failure condition met it is adjudicated VOID; without such an observation the member stays
   open until the observer has read the source (the NO needs evidence of absence, not the absence of evidence).
   An **observation of absence** is either an archived read of the source at or after deadline + window showing
   no matching record, or, when the observer first reads the source later, an archived read in which the earliest
   matching record carries an evidenced publication time after deadline + window: under the source rule's
   **monotone-publication condition** (OD5: the source publishes records with evidenced times and never removes
   one) that record is itself the evidence that no record existed at finality. In both cases the member is NO at
   deadline + window with `knowable_at` = that time, the late record is a source-latency defect and the observer
   outage an integrity incident, so the disposition is the one a read at finality would have reached and never
   depends on when the observer read (RC-113; version 7 left this combined case open, and T-04's "resolves by
   content" could not be applied: YES ignored the latency rule and NO lacked the observation). A source that
   cannot evidence publication times cannot evidence absence: with the observer down at finality such a member is
   adjudicated VOID under the source rule's failure condition, and such sources are an OD5 exclusion at
   registration (fixture F-02e). The invariant is that a member's disposition is a function of the archived
   source records and the pre-registered source rule alone.
   A report of timely occurrence received after the member is final is a **source-latency defect** logged
   against the source: it changes no outcome, is never YES and never VOID, because version 6's "resolves VOID"
   asked an append-only ledger and a committed increment to be rewritten (T-04 tested the NO; the same text
   required the VOID). The per-source defect rate is reported in the protocol report, and a source whose rate
   exceeds its pre-registered bound is withdrawn from new registrations (OD5). The latency window is therefore pre-registered per source long enough that such a report is a source failure. **The
   defect is carried into the statistics, not only counted (RC-145):** the graded disposition stays immutable in the
   ledger and the defect record is appended beside it; the primary scores a member with a recorded defect at the
   outcome least favourable to FULL of its graded and its evidenced outcome, as an early-knowability breach is scored
   (RC-135); it is imputed at both outcomes in the worst-case bounds, is outside the VOID cap and is reported by count
   and source. A defect recorded after the analysis date T_A is reported in a dated amendment of the protocol report
   with the primary recomputed under this rule, and a claim that no longer holds is withdrawn by that amendment; the
   monitor's committed factors are not rewritten, and the monitor confirms nothing. Version 12 said the bias such a
   failure can cause "is bounded by the reported defect count". The count does not bound the size, because one defect
   can reverse an increment: with p_FULL = 0.1 and p_baseline = 0.9 a timely YES graded NO turns X = −0.8 into +0.8,
   and on that null defects on 0.5 %, 1 % and 2 % of members (always on a YES, since a NO cannot be late) raise the
   primary's rejection rate from 5.1 % to 8.0 %, 12.0 % and 24.7 % (from 1.7 % to 3.0 %, 4.9 % and 11.7 %
   at c* = 2.254); scored at the least favourable outcome the rate equals the no-defect rate on both laws run (REPRODUCED,
   `16` Appendix P). As for RC-135, the block statistic is not monotone in one increment, so this is a simulated
   guarantee on the committed laws, carried as a T-12 family.
   *Resolution rules (RC-11, RC-71).* Each EventSpec names its source's pre-registered occurrence-time rule.
   YES requires evidence that the occurrence met the deadline. A reported occurrence with no occurrence time
   and no evidence of lateness resolves VOID (counted against the cap and carried by the worst-case bounds,
   because only a YES can lack a time, so this VOID is outcome-dependent); `EXPIRED_NO` requires evidence that
   the occurrence was after the deadline or that the event had definitively not occurred by it (F-02a, F-02b).
   Sources that do not publish occurrence times are a matter for OD5 (exclusion at registration), not for
   resolution.
   *Point-in-time store (RC-79).* A synthetic point-in-time store with (entity, feature, value, `observed_at`,
   `available_at`, vintage id) and an **as-of read interface** that takes the forecast's evidence cutoff t_cut
   and refuses, deterministically, every read whose `available_at` is after t_cut. The interface, not a
   statistical detector, is the leakage control named by `AGENTS.md` §8; the detector of T-15 is the second
   line.
   *As-of semantics (RC-86).* The read at t_cut returns, for every `observed_at` that has at least one row with
   `available_at` ≤ t_cut, the row with the greatest such `available_at` (the latest vintage an agent at t_cut
   could have read), together with its vintage id, and a typed empty result, distinct from a refusal, when no row
   is eligible; first-release semantics would be a different, pre-registered choice that changes the oracle table
   and not the interface. Refusing rows with `available_at` > t_cut is necessary and not sufficient: a reader
   that refuses every read, or returns the oldest eligible vintage, passes every refusal case and fails the
   oracle, and one that filters on `observed_at` fails both (fixture F-25; REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix I). Built
   only after OD9a, with T-15.
2. **Ledger and registry.** Single transactional writer (SQLite in one process) with canonical JSONL export;
   server receipt time on every admission; append atomic with state change (no phantom records);
   deterministic replay of registry, cluster, capability and e-process state; anchor = committed-prefix
   digest with an explicit unanchored-window report; restore from a clean checkout in CI.
   *Candidate log (RC-78).* The registry holds the eligible-event candidate log of §6: every candidate
   considered is recorded with id, source, time, decision and reason **before** any forecast on it exists; a
   registration without a prior candidate entry, and a candidate entry written after a forecast on the same
   event, are refused; the log is part of the ledger and of the replay (T-37).
   *Anchor trust boundary (RC-34).* A digest only detects tampering if the copy it is compared against cannot
   be rewritten by the ledger writer. Anchors are therefore emitted through an `AnchorStore` interface whose
   production implementation is held by a principal other than the writer (owner-held signed record, or an
   append-only store the writer has no credential for; which one is part of OD6). In Phase 1 the external
   store is a test double that the writer cannot open for writing (separate directory with a different OS
   principal in CI, or an in-memory store owned by the test harness), plus an export of every anchor as a
   record the owner can commit to Git. Verification against a local anchor copy is reported as
   **self-attested**, never as verified; the restore test names which anchor it verified against.
   *Clock (RC-37, RC-65, RC-73).* Every clock-dependent check, admitted or rejected, persists (wall time,
   monotonic time, boot id) in the same transaction before it returns its verdict, and the persisted wall time
   is a high-water mark: a check whose wall time is below it fails closed and is logged. A boot-id change, or a
   wall-minus-monotonic offset that differs from the **baseline persisted at the epoch start** by more than the
   pre-registered tolerance (RC-100: measured against the previous check, a sequence of slews each below
   tolerance keeps an expired capability valid indefinitely, 40 admissions after real expiry in the toy of `16`
   Appendix J; against the baseline the third slew is a discontinuity), is an **unverifiable discontinuity**:
   every outstanding capability is invalid until the owner signs a fresh epoch (re-authorisation), and the
   discontinuity is logged. Two earlier wordings are withdrawn with evidence (`16` Appendix F and G): a
   watermark advanced only by admissions admits an expired capability after a rollback (RC-65), and no rule that
   reads only the wall clock can see an idle window before a rollback or a reboot (RC-73). From Phase 2 the
   `AnchorStore`'s signed timestamp bounds elapsed time at every anchor.
   *Epoch rotation (RC-74, RC-103).* The capability epoch is rotated at every process start, and at **every
   demotion** in the same writer transaction, and each rotation is anchored before any capability is checked,
   so that a capability signed for an earlier epoch is stale after any restore and after any demotion (an unused
   second capability signed in the pre-demotion epoch re-promoted the gate under version 6; REPRODUCED, `16`
   Appendix J); item 5 states the acknowledgement rule that goes with it.
   *Demotion without the anchor store (RC-123).* The rotation on demotion needs the store, and version 8 defined no
   outcome when the store is unavailable. The demotion is persisted locally in the writer transaction with its
   rotated epoch and takes effect at once; until the new epoch is externally anchored the gate is in the state
   **demoted-unanchored**, in which every promotion and every protected action is refused (the T-30 rule for an
   unavailable store applied to the gate as a whole), the anchor is retried, and the state clears when it succeeds.
   Every process start begins at the gate floor, and a promotion needs a fresh capability in the new epoch, so a
   ledger restored to an anchor that predates the demotion revives no level. In the toy of `16` Appendix L, rolling
   the demotion back left the gate elevated and the pre-demotion capability acted once the store returned;
   committing it unanchored staled that capability (the start rotation of RC-74 stales it after a restore as well,
   so that part of the finding does not reproduce), but a restore to the last anchor revived the elevated level and
   a fresh capability acted at it with no promotion recorded; under this rule neither acts and the level after the
   restore is the floor. The alternative, a level that persists across starts, keeps the restore residual and is
   recorded for the owner under OD10.
3. **Statistics kernel.** Fixed-horizon paired comparison of cluster increments at pre-registered cut-offs
   with a pre-registered calendar-block dependence model (the primary confirmatory test of the §6 average-effect
   estimand); e-process consumer fed only by registry resolutions, one increment per outcome cluster,
   predictable λ (archived GRAPA rule, λ_max = 0.5), running maximum (the anytime-valid monitor); proper scores
   (Brier, log clipped at a pre-registered bound); IUT evaluation over a declared comparator set; α-spending
   ledger; shrinkage estimator for rare probabilities (Beta posterior for cohort data, logit shrinkage of a
   model estimate with a pre-registered weight function; built only after OD9a, see A0).
   *Shrinkage of the interval (RC-58).* The estimator maps the model's declared (low, point, high) and the
   cohort (s, n) to (low′, point′, high′) by the same logit-space convex combination with the same weight
   w(n), applied endpoint-wise: low toward the posterior 5 % quantile, point toward the posterior mean, high
   toward the posterior 95 % quantile; endpoints are clipped to [10⁻⁶, 1 − 10⁻⁶] before the logit. Each map is
   monotone and the three targets are ordered, so low′ ≤ point′ ≤ high′ whenever the inputs are ordered; a
   collapsed model interval acquires the reference-class spread, and at n = 0 the model interval is returned
   unchanged. The output interval remains a declared ambiguity set with no coverage claim (RC-05).
   *What each test tests (RC-49, RC-50).* The e-process is a test of the **sequential conditional null**
   E[X_k | F_(k−1)] ≤ 0 for every k in record order, where F_(k−1) is generated by the pre-registered design and
   the increments of the clusters that precede k in record order, (H_k, registration id), the filtration in which the
   monitor computes λ_k (RC-133). Version 10 defined F_(k−1) here as everything recorded before the cluster's
   outcome-knowable time. A predecessor B with H_B = 50 that resolves at 50 lies outside that set for a cluster A
   knowable at 10 with H_A = 100, and inside the record-order one. When A's sign follows B's with probability ρ, the
   chronological null holds on every path while the record-order null fails, and the monitor crosses in 39 %, 79 %
   and 94 % of paths at ρ = 0.75, 0.9 and 1, against 1.8 % at ρ = 0.5 (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix N). Ville's
   inequality bounds the crossing rate under the record-order null only. A crossing is therefore evidence against
   that null, and dependence between a cluster and its record-order predecessors is one way for it to fail with no
   skill. Every monitor null generator (T-39, F-22, F-23, F-26) is stated and simulated in record order. The conditional null is stronger than the §6 estimand's null (population-average Brier
   difference ≤ 0): it is false, while the average null is true, whenever skill varies over time or clusters
   open at the same time share a shock. Under a two-state macro regime the cluster-level e-process crosses in
   28.9 % of 20,000 paths, and under skill that is +0.12 for the first sixty clusters and −0.12 for the next
   sixty (average exactly zero) in 62.8 %; the valid clustered null gives 0.055 % (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix
   D). A crossing is therefore evidence against the conditional null only, is labelled as such, and is never
   alone a confirmatory result or a promotion trigger. The fixed-horizon test addresses the average estimand
   directly; its variance uses calendar blocks as the dependence unit (period-level shocks are the dependence
   that pre-declared outcome clusters cannot capture), with the block length pre-registered in the Phase 2
   contract from reference-class history, never from registered outcomes. Its limits are stated rather than
   hidden: with independent period shocks the block-of-one-period test holds size (5.1 %) where a naive
   cluster-iid variance does not (13.4 %); with a persistent regime (P(stay) = 0.9) **no tested block length
   holds size at 120 clusters over 24 periods** (14.8 % to 27.1 %), which is a supply and horizon question for
   OD4 and OD5, not a property any Phase 1 code can fix (`16` §4.1, Appendix D).
   *The primary statistic (RC-69).* The point estimate is the mean over clusters, θ̂ = (1/N) Σ_k X̄_k, which is
   the §6 estimand; calendar blocks enter **only the variance**, in the block-sum (cluster-robust) form
   Σ_b (Σ_{k∈b} (X̄_k − θ̂))² / N² · G/(G − 1) on G − 1 degrees of freedom, where **G is the number of occupied
   blocks** (blocks with at least one eligible cluster; RC-97). **A cluster belongs to the calendar block that
   contains its maturity H_k** (RC-129; H_k as defined under *Monitor order* below), the value recorded in the registration record before any forecast exists
   and independent of every outcome; blocks are half-open UTC calendar intervals [start, end) fixed in the contract,
   and no other timestamp (registration, `knowable_at`, receipt, adjudication) assigns a block. Version 9 named no
   timestamp: on independent clusters each of the four fixed choices holds size (5.1–5.3 %) but choosing among them
   after the outcomes gives 7.6 %, and when the shared shock follows the maturity month a fixed registration,
   knowable or receipt assignment gives 19.5–20.7 % against 5.3 % for maturity (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix M), so the
   timestamp and the dependence model are one OD4 choice and maturity is the default because it is the
   outcome-independent time closest to resolution. An empty period contributes nothing to the sum
   and is not counted in G or in the degrees of freedom, and the contract pre-registers a minimum G_min of
   occupied blocks below which the primary is not evaluable (OD4). Counting empty periods in G gives 5.8–6.7 %
   size on the valid null at Poisson supply of 0.5 to 2 clusters per period, outside the A3 bound, where
   occupied-only counting gives 4.9–5.6 % (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix J); Appendix F could not show this because
   it forced every period to at least one cluster. A sample whose block sums are all equal has zero variance
   and **never rejects** (the convention of `16` Appendix G, now a T-36 oracle). A t test over equal block means
   estimates a period-weighted quantity instead: on blocks of sizes 1 and 9 with values +0.5 and nine times
   −0.1 it gives +0.20 where the per-cluster estimate is −0.04, and when supply covaries with the period shock
   the two statistics test different hypotheses (88 % against 5.7 % rejection; REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix F).
   Under supply–shock covariance the per-cluster estimand itself contains the shock, so OD4 names the estimand
   (per cluster, as §6 says, or period-balanced) together with the dependence model.
   *What the size bound means (RC-72, RC-80).* The primary test's size is **calibrated on the pre-registered
   null families of T-12; it is not finite-sample valid for every bounded-mean null.** On a two-point
   period-shock null where every cluster in a block shares one outcome and the block increment is +0.903 or
   −0.897 with mean zero, the one-sided t over 24 blocks has exact size 7.33 %; over zero-mean two-point nulls
   it reaches 15.2 % at 24 blocks, always at the rare-catastrophe shape (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix G). No other
   kernel repairs this at the design's horizon, and the statement is a theorem, not a simulation result
   (`16` Appendix H): any test that is level α against **every** mean-zero law on [−1, 1] has power at most
   α(1 + δ)^G against every alternative with mean δ, because the mean-zero mixture (1 − ε)·P₁ + ε·δ₋₁ with
   ε = δ/(1 + δ) produces a P₁-distributed sample with probability (1 + δ)^−G. Over G = 24 blocks that is 8 %
   at δ = 0.02, 16 % at δ = 0.05 and 32 % at δ = 0.08; 80 % needs δ ≥ 0.1225, or 141 blocks at δ = 0.02. The
   Phase 2 contract therefore pre-registers the null families it is calibrated on, a cap d_max on
   |p_FULL − p_baseline| for forecasts entering the primary and a reference-class bound on the share of
   catastrophe periods (the skew bound; OD4), reports the two-point families with their exact size, and reports
   the bounded betting test over block means beside the primary as the distribution-free check with its power
   stated (zero at every useful effect over 24 blocks for the archived kernel; at most the bound above for any
   kernel).

   kernel is still called a one-sided α = 0.05 test leaves the nominal claim unsupported on an allowed model.

   as code** with a parameter box Θ (OD4): block length; the forecast-pair law under d_max; the within-block shock

   VOID:** a member flagged VOID in the **cluster registration record** itself, which fixes the member list and
   the VOID set together; the freeze instant t_freeze,k is the registration record's receipt time, and every

   fixed (version 6 froze the set at the earliest `not_before` and let forecasts precede it, so a party could
   read the paired forecasts and VOID the clusters where FULL looked unfavourable, RC-87; the registration

   mean over the **scored** members, so the cluster keeps weight one with fewer members; **t*_k is taken over

   *α-spending semantics (RC-44).* The ledger reserves and irreversibly debits α_j when the j-th promotion
   test is **registered**, before its evaluation data are unsealed; a failed, non-rejecting, cancelled or
   interrupted test keeps its debit, and j indexes attempts, not promotions. A debit that happened only on
   success would let a proposer run unlimited challengers at no cost.

   maturity H_k = max over its members of deadline + declared latency window is known at registration and does
   not depend on any outcome; ties are broken by registration id, and the **record order** is the
   lexicographic order of (H_k, registration id). A closed cluster is buffered until every cluster that precedes
   it in record order is final, equal maturities included (RC-125: version 9 waited only for clusters with a
   smaller H_k, so two clusters sharing H_k were consumed in finality order, which is outcome-timed; with integer
   month maturities that happened on every one of 2,000 random paths, and the record-order rule adds 0.18 months
   of mean delay; REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix M), the buffered set is reported, and the betting fraction λ_k is the
   archived rule applied to the increments of the clusters that precede k in record order, so that it is measurable
   with respect to the H-ordered past and the conditional null of this document is stated in that filtration.

   cluster registered later with a smaller maturity entered after a factor that the declared filtration says it

   order-free final product (REPRODUCED, `16` Appendix J). Knowable order, which Codex proposed, is therefore not
   a sufficient remedy; the fixed-horizon primary is order-free and unaffected. **Dependence between clusters discovered after registration is never a
   ground for VOID or merge** (RC-51): any such adjudication after outcomes can be known would let an

## 7. Tests specified now and built in later phases

**Credal LP (Phase 5, RC-10, RC-32).** T-25: small exact credal instance; the inner LP over the whole declared
credal set returns a worst case ≤ any sampled-subset worst case; conditional bounds enter as linear constraints
l·P(A) ≤ P(A ∩ B) ≤ u·P(A) with declared zero-mass semantics. Not part of Phase 1 (§3, out of scope).

**Forecast issuance before Phase 4 (RC-106).** No arm issues a forecast on a registered event before every
adapter of the comparator set operates (Phase 4 of `12`), because `AGENTS.md` §2 item 1 admits no forecast with
fewer than three model outputs plus Jev triage and RC-17 requires those inputs for every issued forecast. Phase 2
collects candidates (T-37), registers events, measures supply, and commits Design C **base rates as reference
data** in the ledger; they become the baseline arm's forecasts only when issued inside an issuance window that
opens in Phase 4. An exploratory single-arm track record in Phase 2 is possible only if the owner amends §2
item 1 for that purpose (`OWNER_DECISION`, OD9b); until then no such record exists.

price, non-multiple lot (fixture F-11); not constructible or rejected before any transport object exists. The
typed-builder principle applies in Phase 1 only to the research schemas (T-26, T-29).

**Evaluator side channels (Phase 6).** Exfiltration canaries in evaluator-only data; logs, crash dumps,
telemetry and parser errors scanned for canaries; bounded feedback (pass/fail plus coarse diagnostics); cap on
adaptive proposals per period; evaluator and scorer are separate processes so a submitted artefact never
receives labels; no executable deserialisation or template evaluation of artefacts.
